# Hi, I'm Gordon.

**I'm a UX researcher who does the quantitative work most mixed-methods teams borrow a data scientist for, and the qualitative work most quant researchers hand off.**
{: .lede}

I have a PhD in social and personality psychology from UBC (2024), and I spent the last 18 months as the quantitative specialist on Google's Android Auto UX Research team, on contract. Next is a contract quantitative UX research role at Meta, working on global brand-awareness surveys. I live in San Francisco, and I'm looking for a full-time home in quantitative or mixed-methods UX research.

## What I'm good at

I design studies backward from the decision. I start with the choice a team has to make and work back to the result format, the analysis, the sample, and only then the instrument. That habit is how two vague asks turned into one survey that changed how an engineering team prioritizes bugs.

I make qualitative data count. When I coded 6,504 open-ended responses before a launch, every code had a rule, two coders, a blind adjudication, a human check, and a quote behind every number. A qualitative researcher could audit any percentage in the report back to the person who said it.

And I say what the evidence can and can't support: weighted inference instead of raw means when the sample doesn't match the population, penalties instead of averages when the decision is about failures, and the limits stated in the readout before anyone asks.

## Selected projects

<div class="cards" markdown="1">

### [Benchmarking an in-car assistant: from bug frequency to bug cost](/projects/assistant-benchmarking/)
I owned a ~1,000-driver survey that changed how Engineering prioritizes assistant bugs: by what they cost satisfaction, not by how often they're reported. Factor analysis, Elastic Net, Shapley importance, and a penalty-reward analysis that showed which failures drivers forgive.

### [Coding 6,504 open-ended responses with an audit trail](/projects/coding-open-ends/)
I led a pre-launch study of privacy controls: 128 unmoderated think-alouds and a coding pipeline that made 6,504 open-ended answers usable, with dual coding, κ .75–.87, blind adjudication, and a 92% human check. The onboarding copy changed before launch.

### [Rebuilding an annual satisfaction benchmark so partner rankings could be trusted](/projects/satisfaction-benchmark/)
I inherited a benchmark of about 1,800 drivers across seven car brands and rebuilt it around weighting to fleet share, real inference, and LASSO key drivers. Partner strategy shifted from new features to reliability.

### [Where attitudes and behavior diverge: 300,000 posts and panel surveys](/projects/attitudes-vs-behavior/)
My doctoral research on why divisive posts win: hundreds of thousands of posts collected by API, coded by people and by a classifier, and joined to a survey experiment. Published in Psychological Science, 2024, with the figures here.
