# About

![Gordon Heltzel standing next to a Mondrian painting](/assets/img/gordon-mondrian.jpg){: .headshot}

I grew up near Louisville, Kentucky, did my BS in psychology at Indiana University, and did my PhD in social and personality psychology at the University of British Columbia, where my dissertation was about political open-mindedness and the social pressures that work against it. It won the 2023 Psychology of Technology Institute Dissertation Award.

For the last 18 months I was the quantitative specialist on Google's Android Auto UX Research team, on contract: survey programs, key-driver analysis, weighting, an annual partner benchmark, and a coding pipeline that let a 128-person qualitative study happen before a launch. I'm now a contract quantitative UX researcher at Meta, working on global brand-awareness surveys, and I'm looking for a full-time home in quantitative or mixed-methods UX research.

I grew up reading Kurt Vonnegut, who taught me two things: people are complicated, and you owe them clear sentences. That is still the job. Find out what is actually going on with users, then say it plainly enough that a team can act on it.

Off the clock: houseplants, re-reading Vonnegut, Oreos dipped in milk, fantasy football, and [photography](https://www.instagram.com/thegordogg). The painting in the photo is a Mondrian, which is also where this site's colors come from.
