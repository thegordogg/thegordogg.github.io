# Selected projects

Six projects, written the same way each time: what the team needed to decide, what I did, what changed, and what I'd do differently now. For the three Google projects I describe decisions, methods, and sample sizes, and I leave out metric values, partner and competitor names, unreleased features, and internal tools; the figures on those pages are illustrative and say so. The doctoral and volunteer work is fully public, figures included.

### [Benchmarking an in-car assistant: from bug frequency to bug cost](/projects/assistant-benchmarking/)
<span class="tag">Google · Android Auto</span><span class="tag">survey design</span><span class="tag">key-driver analysis</span><span class="tag">penalty-reward</span>
Two asks became one instrument and about 1,000 drivers across three panels. Engineering adopted the resulting impact × frequency table and now prioritizes bugs by what they cost satisfaction rather than how often they're reported.

### [Coding 6,504 open-ended responses with an audit trail](/projects/coding-open-ends/)
<span class="tag">Google · Android Auto</span><span class="tag">think-alouds</span><span class="tag">qualitative coding</span><span class="tag">LLM-assisted</span>
A pre-launch study of privacy controls for a personalization feature. I ran 108 + 20 unmoderated sessions and built the pipeline that coded the open-ended answers with two coders, blind adjudication, and a human check. The onboarding copy changed before launch.

### [Rebuilding an annual satisfaction benchmark so partner rankings could be trusted](/projects/satisfaction-benchmark/)
<span class="tag">Google · Android Auto</span><span class="tag">weighting</span><span class="tag">LASSO</span><span class="tag">partner readouts</span>
About 1,800 drivers and seven car brands. I replaced raw means with post-stratification to real fleet share, design-based inference, and key-driver analysis, and paired each partner's numbers with their drivers' words.

### [Where attitudes and behavior diverge: 300,000 posts and panel surveys](/projects/attitudes-vs-behavior/)
<span class="tag">PhD · UBC</span><span class="tag">behavioral logs</span><span class="tag">NLP</span><span class="tag">survey experiment</span>
Most people say they like political open-mindedness, and dismissive posts win online anyway. Behavioral data plus a segment-level survey explained the gap. Psychological Science, 2024.

### [Improving a bias-education website for STEM workplaces](/projects/bias-education-site/)

## Two more, briefly

**Segmenting users to find interface friction (freelance, 2025).** For The Joint Chiropractic I designed custom survey instruments, modeled the responses in R, and used latent profile analysis to find data-driven user segments and isolate where the platform interface created friction. I worked with design and engineering on targeted accessibility and interface changes, and bug and support reports fell 64% after they shipped.

**Smaller studies at Google that changed decisions.** A matched comparison of two digital-car-key types from in-product surveys, restricted to car-phone pairs that supported both, which changed the recommendation to partners. A within-subject driving-simulator experiment (N = 14, Detection Response Task telemetry, mixed-effects models) on assistant displays and cognitive load, where I was the analysis lead. And 16 cognitive interviews on the team's core survey metrics that cut redundant items from an expensive survey and seeded an SEM/IRT validation plan.
